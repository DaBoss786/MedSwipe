Serum osmolality is determined by utilizing a formula which involves the sum
of which three osmotically active substances in the blood:

A.sodium, potassium, glucose.
B.sodium, chloride, urea nitrogen.
C.sodium, glucose, urea nitrogen.
D.albumin, glucose, creatinine.
E.albumin, globulin, urea nitrogen.

ANSWER C

EXPLANATION


Osmolality is estimated by computing the sum of serum sodium (mEq/L) x 2,
glucose (mg/dL)/18, and urea (mg/dL)/3. These three solutes are the major
contributors to osmolality, with creatinine, magnesium, phosphate, and
potassium contributing less. The chloride contribution is taken into account by
doubling the sodium concentration.

A 40-year-old woman has CKD with a creatinine of 2.5 mg/dL. The medication
that will increase the serum creatinine but not alter the true GFR is:

A.captopril.
B.cephalexin.
C.trimethoprim.
D.spironolactone.
E.cisplatin.

ANSWER C

EXPLANATION


Endogenous creatinine and creatinine clearance is the most widely used
surrogate for GFR. In the presence of normal renal function, 90% of creatinine
is filtered, and the proximal tubules secrete 10%. As GFR declines, tubular
secretion may contribute up to 35% of all creatinine removal at levels of 40-80
mL/min. The commonly utilized antibiotic trimethoprim blocks the tubular
secretion of creatinine. Since creatinine is produced at a steady state, the
serum creatinine will rise, but the GFR does not change. Captopril and
spironolactone might alter renal perfusion, causing a GFR change leading to
an altered creatinine level. Cisplatin has a direct nephrotoxic effect but would
not raise creatinine without altering GFR. Cephalexin could increase the serum
creatinine through an acute drug-induced injury to the kidney, such as
interstitial nephritis.
The use of µ-opioid receptor antagonists after radical cystectomy and urinary
diversion is associated with:

A.increased cardiac events.
B.reduced opioid consumption.
C.reduced length of stay.
D.increased hospitalization costs.
E.reduced early readmission for postoperative ileus.

ANSWER C

EXPLANATION


Alvimopan, a peripherally acting µ-opioid receptor antagonist, is indicated to
accelerate upper and lower GI recovery following surgeries that include a
bowel resection. In October 2013, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration
(FDA) authorized an expanded indication based on a Phase 4 randomized
multicenter clinical trial. In this trial, patients receiving alvimopan experienced
more rapid bowel recovery and had a shorter hospital stay than those
receiving placebo. There were no differences with regard to early (< 7 days)
post-op ileus (POI) or 30-day all-cause readmission rates between the two
groups. Alvimopan has been associated with the potential for increased
cardiac toxicity in patients with chronic narcotic use and is therefore
contraindicated in this patient population. In the trial mentioned above, such
patients were excluded, and there were no differences in adverse cardiac
events between the alvimopan and placebo groups. In a preplanned economic
analysis of this study, alvimopan use decreased hospitalization costs by
reducing health care services associated with POI and decreasing hospital
length of stay; total expenses were $2,640 lower per patient for alvimopan
compared with placebo. The study did not address opiate consumption, but it
is unlikely that a µ-opioid receptor antagonist should affect opioid intake.
Instead, it would be expected to affect the peripheral effects of opioids on
bowel motility.

mitosis and then subsequently undergo meiosis to form spermatocytes and
spermatids. These are then released into the lumen of the seminiferous tubule.
Spermatozoa exit the seminiferous tubules and travel through the efferent
ducts to the epididymis. The epididymis consists of four segments: the initial
segment, the caput, the corpus, and the cauda. Along the course of the
epididymis, sperm continue to mature; this process includes the acquisition of
motility and other factors that facilitate fertilization. Mature sperm are stored
in the cauda epididymis prior to ejaculation, resulting in highest
concentrations of mature sperm in that location.

B.COPD exacerbation.
C.narcotic overdose.
D.inadequate neuromuscular reversal.
E.pulmonary embolism.

XPLANATION


This scenario is an example of CO2 narcosis. Systemic absorption of CO2

extraperitoneal (via preperitoneal adipose and connective tissues)

possible, but this would present more acutely after extubation. Pulmonary
embolism is always a concern after pelvic cancer surgery, but the patient more
commonly would have an increased respiratory rate instead of a decreased

A randomized, double blind, prospective, placebo-controlled study evaluating
the effectiveness of a new antibiotic for UTI requires a post-treatment urine
culture only for symptomatic patients. The study design is limited by:
A.confirmation bias.
B.verification bias.
C.selection bias.
D.sampling bias.
E.observer bias.

ANSWER B

EXPLANATION


Verification bias occurs in this study as the study design foregoes testing of a
subset of patients, in particular, those of low risk--such as the asymptomatic
patients in this study--so the effectiveness of the antibiotic is overestimated
by omission of the patients that may have had persistent positive urine
cultures but without symptoms (thus being false negatives). Confirmation bias
occurs when there is dismissal of evidence that appears to go against
preconceived notions, while selection bias is introduced when prognostic
factors are unevenly distributed between the experimental group and the
control group. Observer bias occurs when a trial is constructed with an
expectation of a certain result, which is not evident here. Finally, sampling bias
occurs when patients are enrolled in a non-random manner, and is mitigated
here since the study was randomized and prospectively constructed.

new instrument is inadvertently advanced into the liver. The most likely cause
of this injury is due to:

A.dislodgement of instrument faceplate.
B.forceful insertion of the instrument.
C.lack of visualization of instrument tip during advancement.
D.accidental activation of instrument arm clutch button.
E.defective instrument.



ANSWER D

EXPLANATION


Unlike conventional laparoscopy, a robotic instrument must first be
successfully engaged to the instrument faceplate before it can be advanced
into the operative field. During the routine exchange of a robotic instrument, a
safety mechanism is built into the system that allows for the safe and
automatic return of the instrument tip to 1 mm short of the final position of
the prior instrument. In addition, the precise trajectory of the previous
instrument is saved, allowing the new instrument to return to the same exact
location by simply advancing the instrument once engaged by the faceplate.
This feature is called the guided instrument exchange. Although all
laparoscopic and robotic instruments should be inserted under laparoscopic
view, this safety feature, in essence, returns the new instrument to nearly the
exact same location, thus obviating the absolute need for the console surgeon
to visualize the actual insertion and advancement of the new instrument tip.
Taken together, this guided instrument exchange safety feature prevents “past
pointing” of the new instrument into surrounding vital anatomy, whether by
gentle or forceful insertion. However, once the clutch button of the robotic
arm is reset, this safety mechanism is lost, and the instrument requires manual
introduction under laparoscopic view in coordination with the console
surgeon. In the above scenario, the bedside assistant likely activated the clutch
button accidentally, thus releasing the safety mechanism. Proper and
successful engagement of the instrument to the faceplate would not be
possible if the instrument was defective or the instrument faceplate was
dislodged.

During ureteroscopy for stone extraction, radiation exposure to the patient
and urologist is best reduced by:

A.positioning the C-arm intensifier closer to the patient. B. C. D. E.
B.using an overhead radiation source.
C.increasing magnification.
D.using first image capture.
E.positioning the x-ray tube closer to the patient.
ANSWER A

EXPLANATION


Efforts to minimize radiation exposure to the patient and healthcare team are
important. In general, variables that increase radiation dose to the patient will
increase radiation dose to the operator because of scatter. Scatter radiation is
radiation that spreads in different directions when the x-ray beam interacts
with any substance. Reducing the distance between the patient and the image
intensifier (i.e., positioning the C-arm intensifier, or flat panel, closer to the
patient) results in a shorter distance for the x-ray beam to travel after exiting
the patient to reach the image intensifier, thereby allowing the dose rate of
the x-ray beam to be decreased and lowering the cumulative radiation dose to
the patient and the scatter radiation experienced by the surgeon and other
medical personnel in the room. Bringing the x-ray tube closer to the patient
will increase radiation exposure. Since radiation beams diverge with distance,
the maximum possible distance between the x-ray source and the patient
should be maintained. Collimating to the minimum required visual
fluoroscopy field can result in significant reductions in radiation exposure as
well. Image magnification nearly always results in higher patient radiation
doses. The practice of saving the last image taken, rather than the first image,
to use as a reference point using the automatic last image hold feature can
minimize the need for repetitive fluoroscopic imaging. Other techniques for
reducing radiation exposure during ureteroscopy include using spot images
rather than runs of imaging and avoiding overhead radiation sources. Using
the laser-guided C-arm helps target the reference area and reduce overall
radiation dosage.

angiotensin converting enzyme (ACE) to angiotensin II in the pulmonary
vasculature. This leads to vasoconstriction as well as production of
aldosterone, which leads to sodium and fluid retention. In a bilateral renal
system, unilateral RAS results in the above changes. However, the contralateral
renal unit senses these changes and compensates by secreting sodium and
water. As such, an increase in blood pressure in a two-kidney system is
maintained by an increase in renin and angiotensin. In contrast, RAS in a single
renal system or bilateral stenosis in a bilateral system, sodium and volume
retention are not addressed by a normal contralateral system. This then leads
to a reduction of renin and angiotensin, as the blood pressure is maintained
predominantly by volume increases of the retained sodium and water. In this
context, the only choice which is correct in this example is the one indicating a
normal angiotensin, normal potassium, and elevated sodium. A normal
sodium would not be expected given the increase in whole-body sodium and
water. Similarly, elevated angiotensin would not be expected.




A 53-year-old woman has nocturia despite fluid restriction before bedtime.
Serum sodium is 135 mEq/L. The next step is:

A.voiding diary.
B.water deprivation test.
C.re-check serum sodium after two weeks.
D.start desmopressin and re-check serum sodium in one week.
E.start desmopressin and re-check sodium in one month.



ANSWER A

EXPLANATION
A voiding diary (frequency volume chart) is critical in the evaluation of
nocturia, specifically nocturnal polyuria. Nighttime urine production greater
than 1/3 of the 24-hour voided volume in adults would be consistent with
nocturnal polyuria. Desmopressin is a synthetic analog of vasopressin, which is
secreted by the posterior pituitary to increase water permeability within the
renal parenchyma. It acts on the V2 receptors in renal collecting ducts leading
to water reabsorption and decreased urine volume. A water deprivation test is
not indicated in patients with normal frequency volume chart. Once nocturnal
polyuria has been confirmed on frequency volume charting, serum sodium
should be evaluated prior to, one week, and one month after initiation of
treatment.

B.XRT.

EXPLANATION


Imaging shows prostatic rhabdomyosarcoma confirmed by biopsy. The initial
step, under both COG and European protocols, is to place a urethral catheter if

radical surgical excision was the first effective treatment for
rhabdomyosarcoma, the accumulated evidence from multiple collaborative
study groups has documented the effectiveness of chemotherapy as the initial
treatment; thus, the goal of modern therapy is to initially excise the tumor

clinical response of the patient. For example, if the tumor has continued to
grow or is nonresponsive, then resection may be an appropriate option after
discussion with experienced urologists and review at a multidisciplinary tumor
board. After restaging following initial chemotherapy, local control with
radiotherapy or surgery would be appropriate.

EXPLANATION


The image and clinical presentation are diagnostic of penile herniation, a

A 68-year-old man experiences severe pruritus involving his genitals and

EXPLANATION


The patient’s presentation is characteristic for scabies. Scabies infestation is

healthcare settings. Severe pruritis is the hallmark of scabies, and involvement
of the external genitalia is very common. The characteristic findings on exam

A 72-year-old, cognitively intact man with an orthotopic neobladder and
solitary kidney has bipedal swelling. His creatinine is 4.1 ng/dL and urine
specific gravity is 1.010. Creatinine was 1.8 ng/dL one month ago. A catheter
placed into the neobladder drains 1.8 L of urine and, over the next two hours,
he produces 200 mL/hour of urine. In addition to monitoring mental status
and correcting electrolytes, the next step is:

A.0.50 NS I.V. until urine production is < 3L/day.
B.0.50 NS I.V. until urine production is < 2L/day.
C.NS I.V. until urine production is < 3L/day.
D.oral hydration until urine production is < 2L/day.
E.oral hydration until urine production is < 3L/day.



ANSWER E

EXPLANATION


Hypercontinence (incomplete emptying of a neobladder) is a known
complication of orthotopic neobladders, occurring in up to 25% of patients.
This patient demonstrates post-obstructive diuresis (POD) following relief of
obstruction. Given baseline CKD and solitary kidney, he is at elevated risk of
AKI. Urine output of 200 mL per hour for two hours or greater than 3L over 24
hours is diagnostic for POD. He should have close monitoring of his
electrolytes, mental status, and weight. Spot urine testing can be helpful in
stratifying risk for pathologic POD, with urinary sodium greater than 40 mEq/L
pointing to an increased risk of progression to pathologic POD. Urine-specific
gravity can serve as a rough estimate of urine concentrating ability, with 1.010
being iso-osmotic with serum, 1.020 suggesting kidneys are concentrating,
and 1.000 suggesting the kidneys are inappropriately concentrating urine.
Urine specific gravity less than 1.010 is suggestive of pathologic POD. Given
that this patient appears at this point to demonstrate physiologic (not
pathologic) POD, monitoring and free access to oral hydration is appropriate.
Only patients with cognitive impairment should be hydrated intravenously,
preferably with 0.50 NS. Resolution of POD is defined by urine production less
than 3L/day.




A 65-year-old man has gross hematuria two years after a cystectomy with ileal
conduit for a pT2N0Mx urothelial carcinoma. His GFR is 25 mL/min/1.73 m2
(stage 4 CKD) and cytology is negative. The next step is:

A.renal ultrasound and KUB.
B.loopogram.
C.CT urogram with I.V. hydration.
D.CT urogram with I.V. sodium bicarbonate.
E.looposcopy.



ANSWER B

EXPLANATION


A patient with a history of urothelial carcinoma is at risk for an upper tract
recurrence (reported range from 0.75-6.4%). With the new onset of hematuria,
he must have some imaging of his upper tracts. While a renal ultrasound and
KUB could be safely performed in this patient with a low GFR, these studies
are inadequate to evaluate for urothelial carcinoma recurrence. They may
identify calcifications (on the KUB) consistent with a stone or demonstrate
signs of obstruction (on renal ultrasound). Still, they will not be able to identify
filling defects and are inappropriate tests to evaluate the ureter itself. They
would not appropriately rule out the concern for recurrence, which one would
not want to miss. This patient has poor renal function (estimated GFR < 30
mL/min/1.73 m2) and is at high risk for contrast-induced nephropathy. The
use of I.V. hydration in patients with stages 3a or 3b CKD does not prevent
acute kidney injury from I.V. iodinated contrast - and is unlikely to help this
patient with stage 4 CKD. Similarly, sodium bicarbonate has not been shown
to prevent contrast-induced nephropathy when using I.V. iodinated contrast. A
looposcopy will not give any information about the upper tracts. Loopography
can be used in patients who have undergone cystectomy with ileal conduit as
most ureters are freely refluxing, thus allowing imaging of the upper tracts to
evaluate for filling defects. While MR urography would also provide good
upper tract imaging without the risk of contrast, given the options listed,
loopogram is the best initial step.

A 37-year-old woman has urinary urgency incontinence that interrupts her
workday. Urinalysis is normal. She does not wish to take medications. In
addition to avoiding dietary bladder irritants, she should:

A.void as soon as she feels the urge.
B.perform repetitive rapid pelvic contractions (quick flicks) when she has a
sense of urgency.
C.perform slow, sustained pelvic floor contractions multiple times a day.
D.obtain a urine culture.
E.use an anti-incontinence pessary.



ANSWER B



EXPLANATION


Rapid short pelvic contractions (termed "quick flicks") have been shown to
decrease bladder overactivity and urinary urgency. This treatment is combined
with teaching the individual to delay voiding in response to the urge to void.
The principles behind these bladder drills are an attempt to "retrain" the
bladder to overcome the voiding urgency. Patients are taught not to void "just
in case" or when the urge hits but rather to try to delay the void. Slow
sustained pelvic floor contractions are utilized in the treatment of SUI and are
not as helpful in patients with urinary urgency. With a negative urinalysis, a
urine culture is not necessary. For persistent storage symptoms, consideration
of mycoplasma/ureaplasma cultures, and/or cystoscopy if the patient has a
history of tobacco usage, may be reasonable. An anti-incontinence pessary
would not be helpful for urgency incontinence and might actually exacerbate
the symptoms.
